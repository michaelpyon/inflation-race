# PERSONA.md - Inflation Race

## The person

**Danny Reyes, 31, senior data analyst at a mid-size fintech in Austin.** Renting a 1-bedroom that went up 22% in 2 years. Has a 401k he checks too often and a small bag of ETH he pretends he doesn't. Not poor, not rich, permanently annoyed. His entire online personality is "the economy is a joke and I am the punchline."

This is a sharpened version of the V2 evangelist (28-35 econ-anxious redditor from r/personalfinance / r/antiwork). The refinement, with reasoning: the r/antiwork framing selects for people who resent games about money; the actual sharer is the **econ-doomer meme poster who finds inflation funny, not just enraging**, and who already participates in score-share culture (Wordle grids, Balatro clips). Danny keeps the economic anxiety but adds the 2 traits that actually cause a share: he plays browser games at his desk on a real keyboard, and he treats a grade letter as raw material for a self-deprecating joke. Same audience pool, better trigger profile.

## The job he hires this for

A 150-second desk break that converts his economic anxiety into a scoreboard. He is not hiring it to learn about inflation. He is hiring it to feel briefly in control of a burning pile of money, then to turn the result into content. The grade letter is the product. The game is the delivery mechanism.

## His device

MacBook Pro at a desk, Chrome, full keyboard, sound off by default (office). Second visit might be desktop at home with sound on. His retweet AUDIENCE is 80% on phones, which is why the mobile-gated link must still look intentional and premium on a phone screen.

## 5-second bounce triggers

1. Blank or dead canvas on load (worst case: phone visitor with keyboard-only input and no explanation).
2. Reads like an educational econ game or a fintech lead magnet ("Learn how inflation works!").
3. No visible controls or objective within 1 screen; he will not read a manual.
4. Generic template look (default fonts, bootstrap cards) signaling low effort; he only shares things that make HIM look discerning.
5. A sign-up wall or cookie banner of any kind. Instant close.

## The unprompted-share moment

He finishes a Helicopter Money run, the screen slams down an **S grade in giant pixel type with his final net worth and purchasing power**, and the copy reads like a joke he wishes he wrote ("Survived 2020-2025 with $14,800. Purchasing power: $9,100."). He screenshots the game-over card, posts it with "this is the only economy where I'm upper class," and the COPY SCORE text with the grade letter and link rides along. Secondary share moment: beating a friend's grade on the same era after they posted theirs, which is the loop that makes it spread inside a quote-tweet thread.
